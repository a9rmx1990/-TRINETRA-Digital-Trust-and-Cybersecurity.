Historical payment-plane narrative. The active architecture is Figure 1 in docs/agentic.pdf.

The big change from your PDF is that the deterministic DSP engine is replaced by learned models, and a live call becomes the first stage of the pipeline.

Live call flow

The caller joins from the web app over WebRTC (aiortc on the FastAPI side), and the browser streams mic audio.
The gateway runs voice activity detection and cuts the audio into rolling 2-3 second windows.
Each window goes to the deepfake model and to Whisper in parallel.
Whisper’s transcript goes to the coercion text model, which looks for “digital arrest”, fake escrow and urgency patterns.
Scores update a per-session risk value in Redis every second or so, so a call that turns suspicious midway is caught.
When the customer submits a payment, the gate reads that session score, the text score and the graph score for the recipient, then decides.

Hugging Face models (verify each model card and licence before committing)

Voice deepfake: fine-tune facebook/wav2vec2-base or microsoft/wavlm-base-plus for binary real/spoof classification. Train on ASVspoof 2019/2021, WaveFake, In-the-Wild and Fake-or-Real. There are community checkpoints on the Hub (search “deepfake audio detection”) for a quick baseline, but fine-tune yourself for any claimed accuracy. Phone codecs (8 kHz, compression) hurt spoof detectors badly, so augment training data with codec and noise simulation.
ASR: openai/whisper-small through faster-whisper, or distil-whisper if you need lower latency. Whisper handles English, Hindi and Kannada.
Text coercion: fine-tune xlm-roberta-base for multilingual scam-intent classes (authority impersonation, escrow routing, urgency). Add a zero-shot NLI model such as MoritzLaurer/deberta-v3-large-zeroshot-v2.0 as a fallback for scam scripts you haven’t trained on. You will likely need to generate and hand-label scam transcripts yourself.
Graph: keep NetworkX for cycles and 5-hop search, and add a small GNN (PyTorch Geometric) trained on synthetic mule-ring data to score accounts. Keep the deterministic checks too, because they are explainable.
Optional extra: speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb for speaker verification against the account holder’s enrolled voice.

Risk fusion
Replace the fixed weights (0.45 / 0.40 / 0.15) with a small model, such as logistic regression or gradient boosting over the engine scores, calibrated on labelled scenarios. Keep the hard overrides (quarantined recipient, very high single-engine score) as rules on top, since they stop an attacker from diluting one strong signal.

AI-based SOC console

Case triage: a model ranks the alert queue by predicted fraud probability and expected loss, instead of a time-ordered list.
Copilot: an LLM (Claude via API, or an open model) writes a plain-language case summary from the structured evidence: transcript highlights, deepfake score over time, the mule subgraph and why the policy fired.
Natural-language queries: the analyst asks something like “show accounts linked to this recipient in the last 24h”, and the LLM turns it into a graph or DB query that runs against the store.
Feedback loop: every RELEASE or CONFIRM_FREEZE becomes a labelled example, and it periodically retrains the fusion model and the text classifier.
Guardrail: the LLM explains and recommends, but the HOLD/ALLOW decision stays with the scoring pipeline and the analyst, never the LLM.

Latency
Neural models won’t hit the 1.65 ms figure in your PDF, so split it into three paths:

Payment gate: under 150 ms. It only reads the precomputed session score, the graph score and a fast text score.
Call stream: 300-800 ms per window, running continuously during the call, before the payment is submitted.
SOC copilot: a few seconds, asynchronous, and never on the payment path.
